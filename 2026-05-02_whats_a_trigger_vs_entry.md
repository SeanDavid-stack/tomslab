**Trigger vs Entry**

A trigger is a location where a trader can enter a trade with high confidence. It's not necessarily an entry point, but rather a signal to take action.

[[▶ 1:16:13 · Volume Profile + Order Flow:…]]  "The triggers are retracements to high volume. They are fractals of what we're doing in higher volume locations." (Tom video  ·  Volume Profile + Order Flow: Trade Plan + Execution Part 3 w…  (76:13))

A trigger is often accompanied by a specific entry point.

[[▶ 11:38 · Volume Profile + Order Flow;…]]  "You break below. Now you have a trigger in this... You're aligned with that microcomposite volume point of control, which is right here." (Tom video  ·  Volume Profile + Order Flow; THINK like a Retail Trader, DON…  (11:38))

The entry point is where the trader actually enters the trade.

[[▶ 1:16:13 · Volume Profile + Order Flow:…]]  "So if I get above this, this is resistance. So I'm looking in this area because it's an outside edge... And what happens here is either we're going to test, and this is my resistance." (Tom video  ·  Volume Profile + Order Flow: Trade Plan + Execution Part 3 w…  (76:13))

In some cases, a trigger may not have an entry point.

[[▶ 22:22 · Structured Trading in Auctio…]]  "It's a location because we have the gap, you see? And again, it's countertrend. But this is a trigger without entry." (Tom video  ·  Structured Trading in Auction Markets - The Secret Sauce - w…  (22:22))

A trigger can be used to anticipate retail trader behavior and a possible reaction.

[[Mean Reversion · p3]]  "we would anticipate retail trader behavior and a possible reaction if we test this level." (Mean Reversion Structured Trade  · page 3)

In summary, a trigger is a location where a trader can enter a trade with high confidence, often accompanied by a specific entry point.

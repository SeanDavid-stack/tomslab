Tom B identifies absorption as a critical order flow event where market orders are met and contained by significant limit orders at specific price levels, preventing price movement. ## Understanding Absorption

Absorption, in Tom's framework, occurs when aggressive market participants (buyers or sellers) attempt to push price through a level, but their orders are fully absorbed by passive limit orders on the opposite side, causing price to halt or reverse [[Mean Reversion · p5]]. Tom notes that he does not rely on an "absorption detector" in a mechanical sense, but rather reads the order flow directly to identify these events [[▶ 1:06:56 · Volume Profile + Order Flow …]] [[▶ 47:18 · Live Streaming Futures with …]]. It is not merely the presence of large orders (like icebergs) but observing what happens *after* they fill or cancel that provides insight into their significance [[Tom B. · Jan 3, 2023]] [[▶ 47:18 · Live Streaming Futures with …]].

## Spotting Absorption with Order Flow Tools

Tom primarily uses Bookmap to visualize the order flow and identify absorption [[▶ 14:16 · Live Streaming Futures with …]] [[▶ 47:18 · Live Streaming Futures with …]]. He looks for:
*   **Sellers absorbing buyers:** When market buyers are active (buy delta), but price is going down, it indicates that limit sellers are absorbing those buyers [[Mean Reversion · p5]].
*   **Buyers absorbing sellers:** Conversely, when market sellers are active (sell delta), but price is going up, it means limit buyers are absorbing the sellers [[Mean Reversion · p5]].
*   **Price holding at a level:** If a level is tested with heavy volume and selling pressure, but price stays up, it suggests absorption [[Tom B. · Jul 24, 2025]].
*   **Liquidity and "hook over":** Tom describes absorption where "buyers hook over the sellers," signifying that buyers have taken control after absorbing selling pressure [[▶ 1:22:40 · Tariff Roulette & Order Flow…]].

He stresses that seeing absorption is just "telling us information" [[▶ 47:18 · Live Streaming Futures with …]]; it must be overlaid onto the volume profile and auction market context to be actionable [[▶ 47:18 · Live Streaming Futures with …]] [[AMT · p1]].

## Key Price Levels and Context for Absorption

Absorption gains significance when it occurs at specific, pre-identified levels that act as boundaries or magnets for the auction process. These levels include:

*   **Initial Balance High/Low (IBH/IBL):** Absorption at the Initial Balance Low (IBL) suggests sellers are being absorbed by buyers, which can lead to a reversal or a return to balance. Tom observes, "sellers absorbed ibl which is WHY scale there then if we return those sellers bec buyers" [[Tom B. · Jul 15, 2024]].
*   **High Volume Nodes (HVNs) and Variable High Volume Nodes (VHVNs):** These represent areas of "fair price" where significant volume has traded [[Mean Reversion · p3]]. Absorption around these levels can signal whether the market is accepting new value or rejecting the current price discovery [[▶ 1:12:57 · Trading Chop - Will Test You…]]. If the market returns to check a VHVN from below and it finds buyers, it might be too low [[Mean Reversion · p3]].
*   **Micro-structure and Fractals:** Tom applies the concept of absorption to smaller timeframes and "micro-structure." If price breaks higher out of a "chop, chop, chop" period after absorption, it signals that "this area is too low" and can be a mean reversion long entry back to the high volume node within that micro-structure [[▶ 26:52 · Live Streaming Futures with …]] [[▶ 52:13 · Structured Trading: Discover…]].
*   **Overnight High/Low (ETH High/Low):** These Extended Trading Hours levels can serve as targets or areas for absorption, especially when aligning with higher time frame HVNs [[▶ 1:12:57 · Trading Chop - Will Test You…]].
*   **Developing Volume Point of Control (DVPOC):** If absorption occurs as price returns to check the DVPOC and the DVPOC is moving in the direction of the auction, it can indicate potential higher value acceptance [[60 Trades · p11]].

## Absorption as a Trading Trigger

Absorption is not just information; it provides triggers for structured trades, particularly in mean reversion contexts [[▶ 26:52 · Live Streaming Futures with …]].

*   **Mean Reversion (MR):** Absorption often precedes a mean reversion move. If price finds "too low" (absorption) and breaks high, it can revert back to a higher volume node [[▶ 52:13 · Structured Trading: Discover…]]. The market "goes up to go dwn and dwn to go up" [[Tom B. · Feb 22, 2024]]. When a market finds a price "too high" and pulls back to recheck a "too low" fractal where absorption occurred, it anticipates buyers stepping in again [[Mean Reversion · p5]].
*   **Entry/Exit Points:** Absorption can serve as a buying trigger when "market buyers run them over and we leave the fractal...the market is saying that this price is too low" [[Mean Reversion · p5]]. Conversely, selling absorption at resistance can be an exit signal for longs [[Tom B. · Jul 24, 2025]] or an entry for shorts [[▶ 14:16 · Live Streaming Futures with …]]. Tom emphasizes the importance of scaling out of trades when absorption is detected at specific levels, as seen when "sellers absorbed IBL" [[Tom B. · Jul 15, 2024]].
*   **Risk Management:** When anticipating absorption, especially in tricky areas with passive absorption, a short trader needs to be "on a very tight leash" with stops [[▶ 41:50 · Live Streaming Futures with …]]. Range is critical to ensure enough distance for a trade to become risk-neutral before the next zone of stops [[▶ 41:50 · Live Streaming Futures with …]].

Tom's approach is not mechanical; it requires contextual interpretation: "Narrate: If this then that. If not then what?" [[Tom B. · Apr 26, 2023]]. The action taken depends on "which side of price they are on" [[Tom B. · Apr 26, 2023]].
